# Burnout Insights

## Key findings
- Attrition rises with burnout level: **9.4%** (low), **25.5%** (medium), **31.3%** (high).
- The average risk score follows the same order: 0.31, 0.47, 0.61.
- Between departments, burnout hardly differs (average 0.27 in HR, 0.29 in Sales, 0.30 in R&D), and it does not follow attrition (R&D has the highest burnout and the lowest attrition).

## Business interpretation
Within the workforce, employees with high burnout scores are much more likely to have left. Note that the burnout score is built from overtime, work-life balance, job satisfaction and promotion gap, which overlap with the risk score inputs, so this is a consistency check and not independent proof.

## Business impact
If workload and work-life balance problems are not addressed, they are likely to show up as turnover.

## Recommendations
- Track overtime and work-life balance indicators regularly (for example monthly) as early warning signals.
- Balance workload across teams.
- Pair burnout monitoring with manager conversations, not only a score.
