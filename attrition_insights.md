# Attrition Insights

## Key findings
- Overall attrition is **16.1%** (237 of 1,470 employees).
- **Overtime** is the strongest factor: 30.5% attrition with overtime vs 10.4% without.
- The overtime effect holds **inside every department**: Sales 37.5% vs 13.8%, R&D 27.3% vs 8.6%, HR 29.4% vs 15.2%.
- **Early-tenure** employees leave most: 34.9% in the first 2 years, then 15.5% (2-5 years), 12.3% (6-10 years) and 8.1% (10+ years).
- Attrition differs by department: Sales 20.6%, HR 19.0%, R&D 13.8%.

## Business interpretation
Attrition is not random. It is linked to workload (overtime) at the individual level and to how long someone has been with the company. The difference between departments is not explained by overtime, because the overtime rate is almost the same everywhere (Sales 28.7%, R&D 28.2%, HR 27.0%), and average burnout is similar too (0.27-0.30). R&D has the highest average burnout and the lowest attrition, so department-level burnout does not track attrition either.

## Business impact
Every leaver means hiring and onboarding cost and lost productivity. Losing roughly one in three new hires in the first two years is the most expensive pattern in this data.

## Recommendations
- Review overtime practices, starting where the gap is largest (Sales: 37.5% vs 13.8%).
- Improve onboarding and early check-ins for employees in their first two years.
- Look into Sales and HR specifically to understand what else (beyond overtime) drives their higher attrition. This dataset cannot answer that.

## Caution
These are associations in one sample dataset. They show where to look, not why people leave.
